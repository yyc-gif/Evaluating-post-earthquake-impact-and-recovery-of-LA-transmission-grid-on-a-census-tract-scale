## Is the current figure scientifically effective?

Partially. Its values, cohorts and D3 definition are technically correct. It identifies the large C1 group and original-unit mean contrasts, but readers cannot judge how internally heterogeneous each type is or whether characteristics substantially overlap. Eight independent axes make joint, relative multivariable interpretation difficult. The problem is a loss of analytical function, not a claim that the plotted mean values are false.

The candidate ECDFs show C1's large near-zero spectral-impact mass, considerable HHI overlap, C2's lower baseline/higher constraint deficit and C3's larger baseline deficit. C1's higher SOVI and slightly higher NCV14 do not coincide with the largest modeled baseline burden. These are frozen-model-conditioned descriptive differences, not causal results or observations of actual electrical delivery.

## Important normalization distinction

The historical theme generator is archived verbatim from `59d9001`, at `inputs/HISTORICAL_THEME_GENERATOR_SOURCE.py`, lines 5698-5701. It computes class means, then the mean and sample SD **across class means** (`mu=grp.mean`, `sd=grp.std`, `grp_z=(grp-mu)/sd`). Therefore the historical color intensities should not be described retrospectively as full-cohort z scores.

The candidate retains the *standardized-profile scientific function*, but uses the explicitly requested saved tract-cohort mean and population SD. Zero means the 2,299-tract cohort mean, not an equal average of class centroids. This is a documented normalization change for the proposed display; it does not change fitted features, H2 weights or memberships. Historical and candidate z-score magnitudes cannot be compared as if they had the same reference population.
