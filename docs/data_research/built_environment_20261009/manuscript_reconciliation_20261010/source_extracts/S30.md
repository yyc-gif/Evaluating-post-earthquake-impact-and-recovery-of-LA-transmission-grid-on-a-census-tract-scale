## P0023 / body block 24 | 4.1 Restoration priorities, network propagation and community recovery

4.1 Restoration priorities, network propagation and community recovery


## P0024 / body block 25 | 4.1 Restoration priorities, network propagation and community recovery

The damage-propagation and logistics results together show how restoration sequencing changes both network-mediated losses and community service burden. The Impact-first and Degree-first contrasts illustrate that changes in source-path burden, population burden, hospital-tract burden and recovery milestones need not move in the same direction. Crew scarcity and repair-duration uncertainty control the amount of sequencing leverage.


## P0025 / body block 26 | 4.2 Vulnerability-targeted restoration and between-community outcomes

4.2 Vulnerability-targeted restoration and between-community outcomes


## P0026 / body block 27 | 4.2 Vulnerability-targeted restoration and between-community outcomes

The typology results describe the community conditions that coexist with different recovery patterns, while the Vulnerability-first experiment quantifies redistribution under a targeted priority. Q4 improvement, impacts on Q1 and other groups, aggregate burden, hospital-tract burden, and Q4–Q1 separation represent distinct outcomes. The C29/C57 comparison shows stronger redistribution when repair resources are more constrained.


## P0027 / body block 28 | 4.3 Relationship to prior Los Angeles studies, historical context and applicability

4.3 Relationship to prior Los Angeles studies, historical context and applicability


## P0028 / body block 29 | 4.3 Relationship to prior Los Angeles studies, historical context and applicability

The revised results are compared with prior Los Angeles seismic power-risk and restoration studies by electrical representation, decision problem, and spatial allocation. Northridge milestones provide recovery-timescale context. Differences in customer versus population denominators, network vintage, utility operations, and recovery definitions frame the comparison. The source gate represents modeled upstream source accessibility; local SCE facility evidence shows that source reachability and facility electrical adequacy are distinct quantities.
