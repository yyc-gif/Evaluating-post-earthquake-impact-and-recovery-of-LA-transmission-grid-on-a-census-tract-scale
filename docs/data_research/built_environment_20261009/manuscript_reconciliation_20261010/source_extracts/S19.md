## P0004 / body block 4 | Abstract

Earthquake damage can delay the recovery of electricity service unevenly across urban communities, yet a station repair order that benefits the system average may have different effects on vulnerable tracts. We retain a 92-station, 318-edge abstraction of the Los Angeles transmission network and map its source-connected station availability to 2,315 census tracts. In each of 1,000 physical realizations for each of four hazard fields, damage states and positive repair durations determine tasks and crew occupation; all restoration sequences share the same physical draw. A utility-compatible tract dependency proxy is compared with the submitted mapping and with public Southern California Edison circuit candidates. We decompose cumulative modeled service deficit into station self-damage, functional-threshold loss, and source-path disconnection. Under the 2%-in-50-year hazard and 57 pooled crews, the hospital-first sequence produces 34.306 h of population-weighted modeled burden, including 4.065 h (11.8%) from source-path loss. An impact-first sequence lowers aggregate burden by 0.713 h but raises the source-path component by 0.103 h. A fixed vulnerability-first rule lowers the highest-vulnerability quartile burden by 1.595 h relative to hospital-first, while raising the lowest-quartile burden by 1.269 h and increasing absolute group separation by 2.501 h. Thus targeted benefit, average recovery, and disparity do not move together. The direct community-objective genetic search did not improve its best deterministic incumbent, impact-first. Mapping and gate contrasts distinguish stable aggregate comparisons from more sensitive tract attribution. These are conditional service-availability estimates, not measured customer outages or delivered power.

## P0007 / body block 7 | 1 Introduction

Electric power disruption after an earthquake is shaped by more than the count of damaged components. A damaged station can lose its own functionality, interrupt an upstream path to other stations, and affect tracts assigned to those stations through a spatial dependency model. Limited crews, travel, and repair durations then determine when those states change. These linked processes can make the same hazard produce different recovery paths across communities.


## P0008 / body block 8 | 1 Introduction

Existing studies of earthquake power recovery have advanced fragility, network connectivity, repair scheduling, and community vulnerability analysis. The retained July manuscript reviewed these streams, including community-centered recovery work by Logan and Guikema, Los Angeles earthquake risk studies, and optimization approaches to critical-service restoration. Their combination does not by itself demonstrate community benefit: an early completion objective for stations need not minimize the tract-level deficit created by source disconnection and service dependencies. Nor does a favorable vulnerability-weighted system curve reveal which groups or neighborhoods were delayed. The present comparison evaluates both mechanisms and distributional consequences within one consistent damage, repair, and service model.


## P0009 / body block 9 | 1 Introduction

Three questions organize the study. First, how do station damage and source-path connectivity propagate to spatially different tract service deficits? Second, how do scarce crews, travel, task duration, and fixed repair priorities alter system and community recovery under identical physical realizations? Third, where do long recovery burdens coincide with vulnerability, built-environment exposure, risk, and modeled grid dependency, and who benefits or loses when restoration priority changes? The network is an abstraction and tract assignments are dependency proxies. These boundaries motivate the external candidate benchmark, mapping and gate contrasts, and explicit loss decomposition rather than claims of feeder-level validation or delivered electricity.

## P0014 / body block 14 | 2.2 Damage, tasks, and a single repair clock

2.2 Damage, tasks, and a single repair clock


## P0015 / body block 15 | 2.2 Damage, tasks, and a single repair clock

Damage states DS0–DS4 determine both initial residual functionality and task eligibility. Only DS>0 stations enter a realization’s queue. Each damaged station receives one positive draw from its retained DS-specific repair-duration scenario; DS0 duration is zero. A strategy is a complete ex-ante 92-station order, filtered by deleting DS0 stations without reranking. A crew becomes available at t=0 at its retained yard. Each dispatch uses directed base-to-task or previous-task-to-task road travel. Arrival equals crew availability plus travel; completion equals arrival plus that task’s realized duration. The crew is released and raw station functionality is restored at that same completion event. There is no independent post-schedule continuous recovery clock. Within each physical realization, strategies share DS, durations, crews, roads, graph, sources, and mapping; only their order differs.


## P0016 / body block 16 | 2.3 Source-connected availability and cumulative burden

2.3 Source-connected availability and cumulative burden


## P0017 / body block 17 | 2.3 Source-connected availability and cumulative burden

For station i at event time t, let f_i be raw functionality, F_i indicate whether f_i is at least 0.5, and C_i indicate connectivity through functional stations to an active retained Core source. Effective availability is e_i=f_i F_i C_i. We use event-exact left-rectangle integration between distinct completion times. This binary path model does not impose MW balance, thermal ratings, voltage, dispatch, or load shedding. Dynamic source counts, path-disjointness, cuts, bridges, and articulation dependence describe redundancy of the modeled graph; they are not electrical transfer capacities.


## P0018 / body block 18 | 2.3 Source-connected availability and cumulative burden

The identity 1−e_i=(1−f_i)+f_i(1−F_i)+f_i F_i(1−C_i) separates self-damage, threshold, and source-path losses. Tract known-available mass is the M1-weighted effective station state. Let R_r be resolved candidate mass and A_r(t) the known-available mass of tract r. Its cumulative deficit is B_r=∫[R_r−A_r(t)]dt, and normalized burden is B_r/R_r when R_r>0. Full tract population weighting of normalized burdens and population-times-resolved-mass weighting of B_r are distinct estimands; both are recorded. In this 92-station formal mapping, every one of the 2,315 tracts has positive resolved mass. The same formulas retain an unresolved/NA category for less complete mappings rather than treating missing service as outage or restoration. Population T50/T80 are crossings of modeled resolved availability, not measures of equity or observed supply.

## P0021 / body block 21 | 2.4 Restoration decisions and direct community search

The July genetic algorithm ranked station-level weighted early completion, with population, hospital, vulnerability, and network-importance terms and a makespan penalty. Candidate fitness did not run source-gated tract recovery; hence its score need not rank tract T80 or burden. The revised 92-station permutation algorithm retains population 100, 100 fixed generations, crossover probability 0.8, inversion mutation probability 0.2, tournament size 3, and five independent seeds 42–46. For a candidate order π, each of 64 independent 2pc50 planning realizations is fully scheduled and propagated to M1 tract availability; the fitness is minus the mean population-times-resolved-mass cumulative burden. These realizations share the fixed 2pc50 PGA field but have different damage and duration draws and do not overlap the 1,000 evaluation samples. Seven deterministic rules are scored on the same planning samples first. Best-so-far retention prevents the selected solution from falling below the best deterministic incumbent. The best incumbent was impact-first; none of the five search seeds exceeded it, so the retained direct-community sequence is exactly impact-first, not an independent GA improvement.

## P0039 / body block 42 | 3.4 Community distribution, targeting, and typology

Vulnerability-first deliberately directs priority toward mapped Q4 population. Relative to hospital-first it lowers Q4 burden by 1.595 h (paired 95% bootstrap interval −1.631 to −1.558), but raises Q1 by 1.269 h and the absolute Q4−Q1 separation by 2.501 h. Population burden differs by only −0.049 h, while T80 increases 0.466 h and hospital-tract burden increases 0.572 h. Relative to impact-first it lowers Q4 by 0.710 h but raises population burden by 0.664 h. Thus the high-vulnerability benefit is real within the model but not an unqualified equity improvement. In the 2pc50 paired-mean tract comparison with hospital-first, 328 tracts (1,325,640 people) improve, 532 (2,104,575) worsen, and 1,455 (5,636,307) are within ±1 h. Within Q4, 551,450 residents are in improved tracts and 139,034 in worsened tracts. These classifications summarize paired mean tract effects, not frequencies of individual earthquake outcomes.


## P0040 / body block 43 | 3.4 Community distribution, targeting, and typology

The Q4 targeting tradeoff depends on resources. At C29, vulnerability-first lowers Q4 burden by 6.385 h relative to hospital-first, but adds 1.710 h to population burden, 6.255 h to T80, and 15.268 h to absolute group separation. At C86/C114, policy differences approach zero. Under the three historical hazards, Q4 gains relative to hospital-first are only 0.004–0.015 h; the larger 2pc50 effect cannot be generalized to every hazard. The Stage 7 descriptive typology retains recovery, vulnerability, built environment, risk, and dependency variables. Its 2,291 analyzed tracts form five clusters in the formal log-transformed specification; 24 tracts are excluded from the clustering input rather than silently assigned. Cluster membership is sensitive to raw versus log-tailed exposure features (adjusted Rand index 0.484), although the top-ten slow-vulnerable hotspot overlap is complete. These clusters screen co-occurring tract attributes; they do not attribute individual recovery times causally to vulnerability or housing.


## P0041 / body block 44 | 3.5 Gate, physical, and optimization boundaries

3.5 Gate, physical, and optimization boundaries


## P0042 / body block 45 | 3.5 Gate, physical, and optimization boundaries

Under 2pc50 hospital-first, removing the gate yields 29.278 h versus 34.306 h under production. The 5.028 h difference is 0.963 h threshold loss plus 4.065 h genuine source-path loss; calling it all disconnection would be incorrect. Threshold 0.05 and 0.75 yield 34.280 and 34.310 h. The main impact-first versus hospital-first ordering survives these evaluated gates and M0/M1, while some near-tied network-rule ranks swap. The separate no-threshold connectivity test confirms that all saved station functionalities remain positive (minimum 0.03); because the 92-station graph is one source-containing component, letting every f>0 station transmit a path makes C=1 and collapses exactly to the ungated G0 case. A continuous capacity model cannot be obtained by simply deleting the threshold.


## P0043 / body block 46 | 3.5 Gate, physical, and optimization boundaries

The direct community genetic search returned the impact-first incumbent in every seed. This is evidence of no improvement within the fixed five-seed, 100-generation budget, not proof that impact-first is globally optimal. The July surrogate and revised direct fitness solve different objectives and their numeric values are not comparable. Hospital-first can perform differently on hospital-tract burden, population T80, and source-path loss because none is identical to the aggregate mean-burden objective.


## P0044 / body block 47 | 4 Discussion

4 Discussion


## P0045 / body block 48 | 4 Discussion

The three study questions have distinct answers. Damage propagates in the abstract network through source-path eligibility as well as local residual functionality. Crew and travel constraints create additional exposure time, and station priorities move both local repair and downstream path recovery. At the community level, a targeted Q4 benefit may accompany delay elsewhere and a wider absolute gap. These are paired decisions under identical modeled earthquake draws, which supports within-model causal comparison of the priority sequence. It does not support claims about measured Los Angeles customer outages, true feeder assignments, or socially optimal allocation.


## P0046 / body block 49 | 4 Discussion

The utility-compatible mapping improves public candidate agreement where the official candidate can be represented, but 480 strict-SCE tracts lack that representation. Both the candidate inventory and 3% cutoff constrain the meaning of local maps. Aggregate strategy comparisons can retain their direction while tract identities and source-loss attribution shift. Source-connected availability is an upstream reachability proxy: the 14 Core points have audited identities, but generation Pmax, power balance, branch thermal limits, voltage, switching, and load shedding are absent. A local SCE planning facility (OLINDA 66/12) has a reported demand-to-limit ratio near 1.09 in its planning convention, illustrating that connection and adequacy are different questions; the number is not an earthquake overload or a rating for one of the 318 abstract edges. A credible DC/AC benchmark would require a separate real bus/branch case, rather than fabricated impedances on this graph.


## P0047 / body block 50 | 4 Discussion

The positive repair-duration parameters and pooled C57 crews are scenarios rather than observed earthquake staffing or calibrated task times. Historical earthquake fields are hazard contexts, not validation against historical restoration milestones. The distributional results are normalized modeled service deficits, not resident hours without electricity. The vulnerability-first policy tests one transparent Q4-targeted decision and does not isolate a complete efficiency–equity frontier. Cluster and hotspot outputs are descriptive screening. These boundaries are material to application: a future operational study would need utility feeder/customer links, switching and demand, crew records, and observed outages before recommending a specific field dispatch order.


## P0048 / body block 51 | 5 Conclusions

5 Conclusions


## P0049 / body block 52 | 5 Conclusions

Using the retained Los Angeles network abstraction and four paired hazard ensembles, the revision makes damage, repair workload, crew release, and station restoration share one event clock. Under 2pc50/C57, source-path disconnection accounts for 4.065 of 34.306 h of population-weighted modeled burden under hospital-first. An impact-first order improves average burden but does not reduce this source component; connectivity-oriented rules can reduce it while worsening community burden. Utility-compatible candidate eligibility improves conditional SCE candidate agreement, though local tract attribution remains mapping-sensitive. Explicit Q4 targeting lowers Q4 absolute burden but delays Q1 and widens absolute Q4–Q1 separation, showing why aggregate burden, targeted benefit, and inequality need separate reporting. The revised direct-objective genetic search retained its impact-first deterministic incumbent. These findings support conditional comparisons of restoration decisions within a transparent service-availability proxy, not predictions of delivered power or a globally optimal policy.
