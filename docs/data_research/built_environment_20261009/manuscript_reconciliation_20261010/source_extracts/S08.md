## P0003 / body block 3 | Front matter

Please consider our manuscript, “Post-Earthquake Power Service Recovery in Los Angeles: Linking Substation Damage, Restoration Logistics, and Tract-Level Disparities,” for publication as a Research Article in the International Journal of Disaster Risk Reduction.


## P0004 / body block 4 | Front matter

Existing assessments of earthquake impacts on electric infrastructure often examine physical damage, network performance, restoration operations, and community service consequences separately. Our study brings these elements together in a census-tract-scale framework for the Los Angeles metropolitan area. The framework models how scenario-based substation damage affects census-tract-level power-service recovery while accounting for network connectivity, tract–substation dependencies, repair logistics, and alternative restoration strategies.


## P0005 / body block 5 | Front matter

The results show that post-earthquake power-service recovery cannot be explained by network centrality alone. Population exposure, tract–substation dependencies, crew constraints, travel logistics, and repair sequencing jointly determine the pace and spatial distribution of service restoration. Strategies prioritizing hospitals and large affected populations show stronger recovery performance than strategies based only on topological importance. The analysis also identifies census tracts where delayed modeled restoration coincides with social vulnerability, older housing stock, and elevated disaster-risk exposure.


## P0006 / body block 6 | Front matter

Aligned with IJDRR’s multidisciplinary focus, the study offers a quantitative framework for comparing system-, population-, and critical-service-oriented restoration priorities and for identifying communities where prolonged power-service disruption may compound existing vulnerabilities.
