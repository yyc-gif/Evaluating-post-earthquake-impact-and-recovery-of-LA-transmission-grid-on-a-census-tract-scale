## P0084 / body block 86 | 4.1 Restoration priorities, network propagation and community recovery

4.1 Restoration priorities, network propagation and community recovery


## P0085 / body block 87 | 4.1 Restoration priorities, network propagation and community recovery

Content. The combined damage-propagation and logistics results connect station repair sequencing with both source-path loss and community service burden. Population-, network- and hospital-oriented priorities do not move all outcomes in the same direction, and the Degree-first contrast provides a clear example of improved source-path burden alongside higher total community burden. Under the tested 2pc50 resource cases, policy contrasts are largest in the most crew-constrained condition and generally diminish as crew availability increases, while longer repair durations amplify Q4 and Q4–Q1 separation contrasts. The transferable mechanism is therefore greater sequencing leverage when restoration capacity is more constrained relative to workload; it is not claimed as a strictly monotonic or universal law.
