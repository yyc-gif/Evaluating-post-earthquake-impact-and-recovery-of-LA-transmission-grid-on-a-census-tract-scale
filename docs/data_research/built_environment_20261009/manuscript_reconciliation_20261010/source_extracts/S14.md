Response to Comments
0. A Nomenclature section has been added near the beginning of the manuscript to
define the principal indices, sets, variables, and units.
1. The geographical description has been standardized to Los Angeles County, and
the exact study area is now defined as the contiguous urbanized portion of the
county south of the San Gabriel Mountains.
2. Inverse Distance Weighting (IDW) is now defined at its first occurrence.
3. Peak Ground Acceleration (PGA) is now defined at its first occurrence.
4. Genetic Algorithm (GA) is now written in full and defined at its first occurrence.
5. Additional justification has been added. The effective distance combines the
off-network Euclidean distance from the tract centroid to its nearest network access
point and the along-network shortest-path distance from that access point to the
target substation.
6. The fragility median and dispersion parameters are adopted from the FEMA
HAZUS substation fragility model. Spatial variation in risk is introduced through the
scenario-specific PGA interpolated at each substation location.
7. The FEMA HAZUS methodology and technical manual are now cited where the
fragility and damage-to-functionality relationships are introduced.
8. The restoration duration is now explicitly defined as nonnegative: it is zero for
undamaged substations and follows a damage-state-specific zero-truncated normal
distribution for damaged substations. It stochasticity lies in Monte Carlo sampling. Its
stochastic treatment represents uncertainty in repair productivity, access conditions,
and site-specific complexity.
9. All terms in the recovery and source-connectivity equations have now been
defined immediately after the corresponding equations, and the raw and
source-gated functionality measures have been separated more clearly.
10. The percolation curve describes static network fragmentation as nodes are
progressively removed, whereas the LCC recovery measure describes dynamic
network reassembly over restoration time. This distinction is now stated explicitly.
11. A clarification has been added that individual road-travel times are generally
smaller than repair durations, but repeated travel remains operationally relevant
because it affects crew availability, task assignment, and repair sequencing.

12. Population, hospital coverage, and SVI-weighted exposure are now normalized
independently across the scenario-specific repair-task set before they are combined.
13. The previous median-based reference value has been removed. The revised
formulation uses independently normalized task attributes and therefore does not
require the arbitrary reference scale.
14. The notation has been revised so that voltage-related quantities and task-priority
values use distinct symbols.
15. The objective-function explanation has been expanded. The product rewards the
early completion of high-priority tasks, while the makespan term penalizes long
overall schedules; both terms are normalized to improve comparability.
16. T80​ is now defined before its first use as the time required for modeled service
availability to reach 80%. It is also included in the Nomenclature and Table 3.
17. The substation-level network metrics are normalized before being mapped to
census tracts. The final tract-level clustering features are then transformed where
necessary and standardized before K-means clustering.
18. The undefined dimension P has been removed. The tract feature vector is now
explicitly defined as an 11-dimensional vector, and the assembled matrix has
dimensions |R|×11|.
19. Table 3 has been expanded to include all 11 clustering variables, their
mathematical symbols, descriptions, interpretations, and applicable transformations.
20a. The general explanation of K-means has been shortened and supported by
references. The revised section now states the 11 input variables, the
standardization procedure, the cluster-number selection criterion, and the objective
of identifying recovery-vulnerability typologies.
20b. All figure captions have been revised to include the complete figure number
and description
21. The relationship between Figures 2A and 2B has been clarified. An edge connects
two substations only when their physical-network path contains no intermediate
registered substation, and its weight retains the corresponding Dijkstra shortest-path
length. The reduced graph is used for connectivity-based analyses and is not
interpreted as a power-flow equivalent.
22. Los Angeles Department of Water and Power (LADWP) and Southern California
Edison (SCE) are now written in full at their first occurrences.

23. The functionality shown in Figure 5A is now explicitly identified as the
population-weighted tract-level service-availability curve, defined in the system-level
recovery subsection.
24. The Figure 5C legend has been revised to identify the black curve explicitly.
25. The unconstrained baseline has been added to Figure 5A and identified as an

26. The manuscript now explains that the unconstrained baseline has the largest
AUC because all substation recovery processes begin immediately and no
crew-capacity, sequencing, depot-travel, or inter-task travel delays are imposed.
27. We agree that the previous statement was expected and did not sufficiently
explain the substantive contrast. The revised discussion now emphasizes that
topology-oriented and service-oriented strategies prioritize different outcomes.
Topology-oriented rules restore substations that are structurally important to graph
connectivity, whereas service-oriented rules prioritize substations with stronger
tract-level population, social-vulnerability, or hospital-related service consequences.
Under the 2%-in-50-year scenario, hospital-first and impact-population-first reach
T80 substantially earlier than the degree-, betweenness-, closeness-, and
impact-λ2-first rules. The revised text further interprets this contrast in relation to
tract-substation dependency and the alignment of each repair order with the spatial
distributions of population exposure and social vulnerability.
28. The manuscript now clarifies that GA-HospitalFirst does not optimize AUC
directly. Its strong hospital-related priority tends to advance repairs at substations
associated with substantial population and SVI-weighted service dependencies,
producing a larger cumulative recovery value as an indirect outcome.
29. Figure 6A has been revised using more distinguishable line colors, line styles, and
widths so that the Cluster 1 recovery curve is clearly visible.
30. Marta C. González has been added to the CRediT authorship contribution
statement as a co-author.
31. The redundant future-work paragraph has been removed.
