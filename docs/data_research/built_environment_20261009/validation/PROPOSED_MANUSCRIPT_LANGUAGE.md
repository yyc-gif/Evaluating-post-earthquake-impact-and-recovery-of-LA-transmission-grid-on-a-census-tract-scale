# Proposed Language for Review Only

## Methods: Supported Now (Option B)

We supplemented the interpretation of the existing residential-tract clusters with descriptive housing configuration, without refitting the clustering or restoration models. Using ACS 2018-2022 five-year table B25024, we calculated the proportion of all housing units in structures containing five or more units. The denominator includes occupied and vacant units. Housing age remained a descriptive measure of historical development, not a calibrated indicator of seismic resistance. Both housing tables were aligned to the existing tract identifiers and denominators.

Derived configuration uncertainty was estimated using the Census Bureau's 80 successive-difference variance replicates, with modeled uncertainty for zero/100-percent estimates. We report equally weighted tract means, medians and interquartile ranges, alongside conditional measurement-uncertainty intervals and sensitivity to 10- and 5-percentage-point MOE screens. Screening subsets were not substituted for the original cluster populations because precision-based removal can change neighborhood composition. These supplementary descriptors were not used to estimate additional earthquake damage, electricity demand or station capacity.

## Results: Supported Now

Housing configuration was defined for all 2,291 residential tracts; 23 tracts in the 2,315-tract full domain had zero housing denominators. The median derived 90% configuration MOE was 6.75 percentage points, and 211 residential tracts exceeded 10 points. Mean 5+-unit shares in clusters 1-5 were 40.25%, 47.35%, 27.56%, 25.82% and 31.86%. The configuration contrast between clusters 2 and 4 was 21.52 percentage points (augmented approximate 90% measurement interval 19.50-23.55; Hedges g=0.753), while clusters 3 and 4 differed only weakly (g=0.066). Within-cluster distributions overlapped substantially.

Screening at 10 points retained 90.79% of tracts and changed cluster means by at most 1.11 points. The 5-point screen retained only 29.25% and disproportionately removed multi-unit-dominant and socially vulnerable tracts; cluster 5 retained only five observations. Configuration correlated moderately with log population density (r=0.493) and housing age (r=-0.449), rather than reproducing either alone. Its limited residual association with the existing labels after adjustment for all original features indicates contextual interpretive value, not evidence for a new or sharply separated community partition.

## Limitation

Complete ACS geographic coverage does not imply precise individual-tract estimates. Reported measurement intervals condition on the existing cluster assignments and do not propagate clustering, restoration-model or all social-indicator uncertainty. The typology remains multidimensional: recovery outcomes, network/dependency measures, social context, composite hazard risk and economic building value must not be relabeled as purely physical urban form.

## Imperviousness: Validation Note, Not Final Results Language Yet

A reproducible pilot using 2022 Annual NLCD numeric data from a public mirror produced valid land-normalized impervious fractions for all study tracts, with explicit water and boundary-cell handling. Pilot mean fractions in clusters 1-5 were 67.72%, 64.83%, 66.23%, 51.76% and 64.29%. Although these measurements showed distinct constructed-surface information, their publication admission remains conditional on verifying an authoritative USGS release because the mirror is labeled beta/non-production and exposes inconsistent item/catalog version descriptions. These pilot values should not yet appear as final manuscript results.
