# Results draft - Joint community typology

The four classes describe distinct combinations of modeled recovery and community attributes. They are retained as C1-C4 rather than assigned archetype names from small or unstable profile differences. Means below give equal weight to each tract; population shares use summed study population. Table 1 provides medians and coverage as well as means.

C1 contains 850 tracts (37.17% of classified tracts) and 3,387,008 residents (37.48% of classified population). Mean service loss is 32.424 h (median 31.212 h), and mean descriptive T80 is 43.706 h. Mean social-vulnerability score is 79.16 (median 81.93). Mean classified-land entropy is 0.283, mean footprint coverage is 26.54%, and mean observed-age pre-1970 building-area share is 84.04%. Median building stock is $324.23 million and median loss-rate percentile is 75.66.

C2 contains 34 tracts (1.49% of classified tracts) and 124,158 residents (1.37% of classified population). Mean service loss is 23.601 h (median 23.077 h), and mean descriptive T80 is 38.503 h. Mean social-vulnerability score is 67.99 (median 85.04). Mean classified-land entropy is 0.384, mean footprint coverage is 24.28%, and mean observed-age pre-1970 building-area share is 71.39%. Median building stock is $389.58 million and median loss-rate percentile is 69.55.

C3 contains 651 tracts (28.47% of classified tracts) and 2,440,001 residents (27.00% of classified population). Mean service loss is 36.339 h (median 36.834 h), and mean descriptive T80 is 46.784 h. Mean social-vulnerability score is 31.06 (median 31.29). Mean classified-land entropy is 0.335, mean footprint coverage is 23.14%, and mean observed-age pre-1970 building-area share is 68.79%. Median building stock is $658.98 million and median loss-rate percentile is 79.14.

C4 contains 752 tracts (32.88% of classified tracts) and 3,085,986 residents (34.15% of classified population). Mean service loss is 33.893 h (median 33.319 h), and mean descriptive T80 is 44.769 h. Mean social-vulnerability score is 82.87 (median 86.23). Mean classified-land entropy is 0.489, mean footprint coverage is 27.69%, and mean observed-age pre-1970 building-area share is 69.15%. Median building stock is $542.52 million and median loss-rate percentile is 89.36.

C1 and C4 both have comparatively high social-vulnerability scores, but differ substantially in land-use mix and observed building age. C1 has lower entropy and a higher pre-1970 share than C4. C3 has the largest mean cumulative service loss and descriptive T80, yet its mean social-vulnerability score is much lower than C1 and C4. Modeled recovery delay and social vulnerability therefore do not form a single ordering. These joint associations do not establish that building form or social conditions caused recovery outcomes.

C2 is the 34-tract subgroup (1.49% of classified tracts). Its mean initial modeled service availability is 1.815%, compared with 0.034%, 0.037% and 0.024% in C1, C3 and C4. It has the lowest mean service loss. Higher initial availability is relative to these other groups: even C2's mean is only about 1.8%, and it is not a continuously powered or universally resilient community class. Its mean SOVI of 67.99 and median of 85.04 also caution against describing all its members as low vulnerability.

Under the 20 spatial perturbation cases, full-cohort ARI had median 0.9234 and minimum 0.7522 for D, and median 0.9458 and minimum 0.8666 for EAL. The corresponding fifth-percentile values were 0.8278 and 0.8728. These summarize tested spatial sensitivity, not percentages of correctly classified tracts or population confidence limits.

The largest small-class expansion occurred for seed 54, deleting blocks 7:-23 and 7:-24. Only four of its original 34 members remained in training. Full-cohort predictions retained all 34, but the aligned class expanded to 92 tracts under D and 90 under EAL. Purity consequently fell to 36.96% and 37.78%; retention remained 100%. This is a sparse-support loss of class specificity, rather than disappearance of the original group. Broad large-class profile differences persist, while some fine rankings reverse: D C1/C4 degree and network-impact differences reverse in 12/20 cases. These rankings should not define class names.

The four unclassified residential tracts contain 12,390 residents. Their known service loss spans 30.813-50.717 h and T80 spans 43.895-63.762 h; each has zero initial modeled service. They remain explicitly outside class summaries, with their measurements reported separately.
