<h1 class="page-title">
  Smart Location Mapping
</h1>
  

      <div class="u-hide-for-print js-toggle-admin-content">
                  <div data-drupal-messages-fallback class="hidden"></div>

  

            </div>
  
        
            
<div>
      <div>
      





  
  

  
  

  
            
  

  <p>This page features interactive mapping tools and data for measuring location efficiency and the built environment.</p><div><strong>On this page</strong>:</div><ul><li><a href="#Background">Background</a></li><li><a href="#SLD">Smart Location Database</a></li><li><a href="#Trans45">Access to Jobs and Workers Via Transit Tool</a></li><li><a href="#walkability"><ins data-date="2021,6,16">National Walkability Index</ins></a></li><li><a href="#calculator">Smart Location Calculator</a></li><li><a href="#Uses">Suggested Uses</a></li></ul><hr><h2 class="highlight" id="Background">Background</h2><p>A large body of research has demonstrated that land use and urban form can have a significant effect on transportation outcomes.</p><ul><li>People who live and/or work in compact neighborhoods with a walkable street grid and easy access to public transit, jobs, stores, and services are more likely to have several transportation options to meet their everyday needs.</li><li>As a result, they can choose to drive less, which reduces their emissions of greenhouse gases and other pollutants compared to people who live and work in places that are not location efficient.</li><li>Walking, biking, and taking public transit can also save people money and improve their health by encouraging physical activity.</li></ul><p>An EPA-funded meta-analysis of the research, <a href="https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/abs/10.1080/01944361003766766">Travel and the Built Environment</a>&nbsp;, summarized the measurable effects of several built environment variables on residents’ travel behavior. These findings can help inform travel demand studies as well as land use and transportation planning studies. However, developing data about these characteristics can be expensive and time consuming.</p><p>To respond to this need, EPA developed four data products that consistently measure the built environment and transit accessibility of neighborhoods across metropolitan regions and across the United States. Each product summarizes the characteristics of census block groups <a href="#_edn2" title>[i]</a>. Users can download data, browse the data in interactive maps, or access the data through web services&nbsp;<a href="#_edn5" title>[ii]</a>.</p><ul><li><a href="#SLD"><strong>The Smart Location Database</strong></a><strong> </strong>summarizes more than 90 different indicators associated with the built environment and location efficiency. Indicators include density of development, diversity of land use, street network design, and accessibility to destinations as well as various demographic and employment statistics. Most attributes are available for all U.S. block groups.</li><li><a href="#Trans45"><strong>The&nbsp;Access to Jobs and Workers Via Transit Tool</strong></a><strong> </strong>provides indicators of accessibility to destinations by public transit. Indicators summarize jobs accessible by transit as well as workers, households, and population that can access the block group via transit. Coverage is limited to metropolitan regions served by transit agencies that share their service data.</li><li><a href="#walkability"><strong>The National Walkability Index</strong></a> provides walkability scores based on a simple formula that ranks selected indicators from the Smart Location Database that have been demonstrated to affect the propensity of walk trips. Currently, the National Walkability Index dataset ranks each block group relative to all other block groups in the United States, but individuals can use downloadable data and the "how-to" document to construct an index for a smaller universe of block groups, like a state, metropolitan area, or city.</li><li><a href="#calculator"><strong>The Smart Location Calculator</strong></a><strong>&nbsp;</strong>is&nbsp;a dataset and interactive tool that measures the location efficiency of workplace locations. Developed by EPA and the General Services Administration (GSA), the interactive map allows users to explore the data, download the dataset, and access the accompanying documentation.</li></ul><hr><h2 class="highlight" id="SLD">Smart Location Database</h2><p>The Smart Location Database is a nationwide geographic data resource for measuring location efficiency. It includes more than 90 attributes summarizing characteristics, such as housing density, diversity of land use, neighborhood design, destination accessibility, transit service, employment and demographics. Most attributes are available for every census block group in the United States.</p><p><ins data-date="2021,6,16">EPA</ins> first released a beta version of the Smart Location Database in 2011. The initial full version was released in 2013, and the database was updated to its current version in 2021.</p><h3>Access the Data</h3><ul><li><a href="https://epa.maps.arcgis.com/home/webmap/viewer.html?webmap=137d4e512249480c980e00807562da10" target="_blank">Interactive map viewer</a>&nbsp;</li><li><a href="https://edg.epa.gov/EPADataCommons/public/OA/SLD/SmartLocationDatabaseV3.zip" target="_blank">Download data for all areas with coverage</a>&nbsp;(ZIP file)</li><li><a href="https://edg.epa.gov/metadata/catalog/search/resource/details.page?uuid=%7B33514B4C-54F2-464A-BCC7-35F441B7E21A%7D" target="_blank">Metadata</a></li><li><a href="https://geodata.epa.gov/arcgis/rest/services/OA/SmartLocationDatabase/MapServer" target="_blank">Web services</a></li><li><a href="/system/files/documents/2023-10/epa_sld_3.0_technicaldocumentationuserguide_may2021_0.pdf" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Smart Location Database Technical Documentation and User Guide</a>&nbsp;has a full description of all available variables, data sources, data currency, data coverage and known limitations.&nbsp;</li></ul><div class="figure image mode-medium">

format</a>.</p><hr><h2 class="highlight" id="Trans45">Access to Jobs and Workers Via Transit Tool</h2><p>The Access to Jobs and Workers Via Transit tool is a free geospatial data resource and web mapping tool for comparing the accessibility of neighborhoods via public transit service. Its indicators summarize accessibility to jobs as well as accessibility by workers, households, and population.</p><p>Coverage is limited to metropolitan regions served by transit agencies that share their service data in a standard format called <a href="https://developers.google.com/transit/gtfs/">GTFS</a>&nbsp;.</p><h3>Access the Data</h3><ul><li><a href="http://epa.maps.arcgis.com/home/webmap/viewer.html?webmap=3bffc086a9b34928a632ab6c8530ebcf">Interactive map viewer</a>&nbsp;</li><li><a href="https://geodata.epa.gov/arcgis/rest/services/OA/SLD_Trans45/MapServer">Web Services</a></li><li><a href="https://edg.epa.gov/data/Public/OP/SLD/SLD_Trans45_shp.zip">Shapefile ZIP</a>&nbsp;(253 MB)</li><li><a href="https://edg.epa.gov/data/Public/OP/SLD/SLD_Trans45_DBF.zip">Dbf (ZIP)</a> <span class="fileinfo">(8 MB)</span></li><li><a href="https://edg.epa.gov/data/Public/OP/SLD/SLD_Trans45.zip">Esri Geodatabase (ZIP)</a> <span class="fileinfo">(120 MB)</span></li><li><a href="https://edg.epa.gov/metadata/rest/document?id=%7B8E9DDDA1-1F38-44F0-9114-CD81DC15019A%7D&amp;xsl=metadata_to_html_full">Metadata</a></li><li><a href="/sites/default/files/2014-03/documents/sld_trans45_ug_0.pdf" target="_blank">Access to Jobs and Workers Via Transit - Technical Documentation and User Guide</a>&nbsp;has a full description of data sources, data coverage (full list of metropolitan areas covered by GTFS) and other known limitations.&nbsp;</li></ul><div class="figure image mode-medium">

id="walkability">National Walkability Index</h2><p><ins data-date="2021,6,15">The</ins> National Walkability Index is a nationwide geographic data resource that ranks block groups according to their The tool provides location efficiency scores for a user-entered address, as well as location efficiency data summarized at the block group level. All of the scores are relative to the region. Other indicators include worker commute greenhouse gas emissions, mode share, vehicle miles traveled, and workplace accessibility via transit.&nbsp;</p><p>Only the Smart Location Index scores and related variables from the 2021 Smart Location Database version are available at this time. This page will be updated when data for other metrics, including transit accessibility and mode split, are available.</p><h3>Access the Data</h3><ul><li><a href="https://www.slc.gsa.gov/slc/">Interactive Map Viewer</a>&nbsp;</li></ul>

<h3>Sample Variables Included in This Dataset</h3><table><thead><tr><th>Dataset Field Name</th><th>Description</th></tr></thead><tbody><tr><td>Score</td><td>Smart Location Index (SLI). Ranges in value from 0-100, where 0 indicates the least location-efficient site in the region, and 100 indicates the most location-efficient site. These scores are relative to the region and should not be compared across regions.</td></tr><tr><td>GHG</td><td>Commute greenhouse gas emissions per worker per year in pounds</td></tr><tr><td>GHG_av</td><td>Average GHG for the region (CBSA or county)</td></tr></tbody></table><hr><h2 class="highlight" id="Uses">Suggested Uses</h2><ul><li><strong>Assessing and comparing neighborhood conditions</strong><br>Users can browse either dataset using a simple interactive map to assess and compare conditions across different neighborhoods in their communities. The Access to Jobs and Workers Via Transit tool includes regional benchmarks to compare the level of transit access of a neighborhood to the highest and average level of access for neighborhoods in the same metropolitan region.</li><li><div><strong>Evaluating and prioritizing investments in location-efficient areas</strong></div><div>Much like energy efficiency, location efficiency reduces resource demands while fostering a healthier, more sustainable built environment and providing equitable access to jobs and services.&nbsp;Location-efficient facilities or public-sector infrastructure investments are generally accessible via multiple transportation options, including:</div><ul><li><div>Public transit and active transportation.</div></li><li><div>Centrally located in a region providing greater access and reduced travel distances.</div></li><li><div>Integrated within a mixed-use environment that offers easy access to services and destinations.</div></li></ul></li><li><strong>Scenario planning and travel demand modeling</strong><br>Planners can use indicators from these datasets as baseline information for scenario planning, sketch planning, and travel demand studies when more detailed or consistent local data are unavailable. Analysts can also use elasticities found in the research literature to adjust outputs of travel or activity models that are otherwise insensitive to variation in the built environment.</li><li><strong>Conducting nationwide research studies and developing tools</strong><br>Researchers use the Smart Location Database and National Walkability Index to analyze the built environment and develop models, planning tools, and databases for use by public health researchers, planners, and elected officials who make decisions about land use and transportation policies, plans, and investments. Research on walkability highlights the relationship between the perception of how walking-friendly a given area is and the quality of life, as well as how walkability potentially influences physical activity.<a href="#_edn4">[iii]</a></li><li><strong>Comparing urban form among metropolitan regions</strong><br>Researchers can use these datasets in nationwide studies that compare metropolitan regions based on urban form characteristics. For instance, analysts could determine the percentage of residents that live in walkable or transit-rich neighborhoods.&nbsp;</li><li><strong>Modeling impervious surface growth</strong><br>EPA analyzed variables in a previous version of the Smart Location Database and the <a href="http://www.mrlc.gov">National Land Cover Database</a> to create a model and simple spreadsheet tool for estimating new impervious surface growth associated with land use development scenarios. This model is sensitive not only to density of development but also to its relative centrality within the surrounding metropolitan region. For details, see EPA’s <a href="/smartgrowth/impervious-surface-growth-model">Impervious Surface Growth Model</a>.</li></ul><div><div id="edn2"><hr><p><a href="#_ednref2" title>[i]</a> Block groups are contained within census tracts and generally contain between 600 and 3,000 people, with an optimum size of 1,500 people. The land area of block groups varies based on population density.</p><p><a href="#_ednref5">[ii]</a> Web services are widely used by geographic information system (GIS) professionals to share geospatial data. A web service is a representation of a GIS resource (spatial data) that a server makes available to other computers or applications. Smart Location Database web services provide access to data layers through <a href="http://www.esri.com/en/arcgis/products/arcgis-enterprise/overview">ESRI's ArcGIS Server</a>&nbsp; and <a href="http://www.opengeospatial.org/">Open Geospatial Consortium (OGC)</a>&nbsp; service formats. Web services are used to display data in the Interactive Map Viewer table of contents and are also available for public use in desktop mapping applications or web-based mapping applications.</p><p><a class="ck-anchor" id="_edn4">[iii]</a>&nbsp;Jaśkiewicz, M., &amp; Besta, T. (2014). <a href="https://doi.org/10.1007/s11482-013-9246-6">Is Easy Access Related to Better Life? Walkability and Overlapping of Personal and Communal Identity as Predictors of Quality of Life</a>&nbsp;.&nbsp;<em>Applied research in quality of life</em>,&nbsp;<em>9</em>(3), 505–516.&nbsp;</p></div></div>
