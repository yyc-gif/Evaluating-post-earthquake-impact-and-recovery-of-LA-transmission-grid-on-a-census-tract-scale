> AUTHOR REVIEW - NOT FORMAL STAGE 7

# Proposed Stage 7 Methods replacement

Community typology was characterized using eight tract-level coordinates organized into three research-question domains. RQ1 included initial modeled service availability, dependency-weighted spectral network impact, and dependency concentration(HHI). RQ2 included equivalent full-service-deficit hours over 0-480 h under Unconstrained recovery and the equally weighted mean additional deficit across five predefined scheduled priorities: Impact-first, Hospital-first, Degree-first, Betweenness-first and Vulnerability-first. The policy weights are descriptive experimental weights, not adoption probabilities, and no average physical repair schedule was simulated. RQ3 included frozen FEMA v1.19 SOVI_SCORE, log1p of frozen formal tract population, and a selected fourteen-hazard geographic screening coordinate.

The hazard coordinate adapts the frequency-area operator of Williams et al.(2024): x_rh=AFREQ_rh(EXP_AREA_rh/AREA_r), using frozen FEMA NRI March2023 v1.19 fields. Each compatible hazard factor was independently min-max normalized using its valid California records, equally summed and the sum min-max normalized among complete fourteen-component California records. Earthquake, coastal and riverine flooding, cold wave, drought, hail, heat wave, landslide, lightning, strong wind, tornado, tsunami, wildfire and winter weather were retained. Avalanche, hurricane, ice storm and volcanic activity had source-null target factors and were omitted without treating unknown as zero. This is a researcher-derived tract-scale extension, not an official FEMA composite or exact replication of the original county-nine implementation. It contains mixed released frequency/probability proxies and geographic conventions and is interpreted as a relative screening context, not multi-hazard occurrence probability, monetary risk or multi-hazard simulation of electricity restoration. Direct SOVI, building-value, resilience, EAL and historical-loss-ratio fields do not enter the hazard operator. Its local variation is strongly influenced by heat/hail/wind source models; source-scale and normalization limitations are documented in supplementary notes.

Many released frequency fields already include area-weighted spatial aggregation. The additional footprint fraction follows the published screening operator and should not be interpreted as an independently measured exposure probability. California reference scaling and the selected hazard set are study-specific conventions, not objectively optimal weights.

The clustering cohort comprised all 2,299 tracts with positive frozen formal population, including eight tracts without conventional housing units. Only16 zero-population tracts were excluded from clustering; all 2,315 tracts remained in study-wide service/distributional calculations. No housing or geometry-based exclusion was inherited. Exact GEOIDs, population values, original vulnerability quartiles and 1,000 frozen physical realizations were unchanged. Features were standardized using the same cohort mean and population SD. Each RQ received one-third squared-distance budget; coordinate budgets were(1/9,1/9,1/9,1/6,1/6,1/6,1/12,1/12), applied through square-root multipliers before equal-tract Euclidean K-means.

Saved fits used K-means++,50 initializations,Lloyd,max_iter=500,tol=1e-4 and seeds 42-46. Existing elbow, full-cohort silhouette, initialization, geographic and policy-set sensitivity evidence supported review of K3 and K4. K3 was retained as the primary candidate for its regional joint profiles; K4, despite higher silhouette, was retained as sensitivity because its added small group chiefly isolates a retained-Core-proxy-linked initial condition, dominated by Q but also including HAYNES/ALAMITOS-linked tracts. Seed42 labels are the deterministic integration snapshot, not an optimized best-seed selection. No new damage, schedule, source propagation or K search was run for this final selection. Candidate class IDs are newly documented within-model labels, not the previous formal five-class IDs.

For the geographic burden display, realization-mean normalized service-deficit integrals were averaged with equal descriptive weights across the five predefined restoration priorities. This mean total deficit equals the Unconstrained mean plus MeanDelta. It is not a simulated average schedule or an expected outcome under a policy-adoption probability distribution. Figure7D displays the continuous deficit in equivalent full-service-deficit hours and outlines the original frozen FEMA SOVI Q4 extent; no additional hazard, vulnerability or burden score is constructed. A supplementary descriptive analysis separately compares original Q4 and non-Q4 using the original five-policy P75 cutoff and comparator-specific percentiles, with predeclared P70/P80, exclusion of Vulnerability-first, original-policy and Unconstrained comparisons. Spatial blocks and a descriptive nearest-neighbor Moran diagnostic characterize geographic dependence; no significant-hotspot or causal inference is made.
