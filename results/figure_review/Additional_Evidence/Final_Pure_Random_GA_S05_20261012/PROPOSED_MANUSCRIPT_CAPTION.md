# Supplementary Figure S05. Optimization from random initialization and independent validation of the frozen genetic-algorithm sequence

The finalized pure-random genetic algorithm (GA) uses 100 independently generated uniformly random station permutations, ordered crossover (probability 0.80), swap mutation (0.10), tournament selection of size 3, and one previous-generation elite under the original validated legacy last-position replacement implementation. No heuristic or previously optimized order is inserted. A passive best-observed archive retains the best actually evaluated permutation. Twenty independent searches each stop at 100,000 distinct evaluated permutations on the same 64 fixed 2pc50 planning realizations; repeated cached queries are not new distinct evaluations.

**(A)** Pale step curves show each run's actual best-observed planning loss, beginning after the 100 initial random orders have been scored. The dark curve is the pointwise median of the 20 observed, right-continuous archives at the union of saved improvement clocks; it is neither generation-average fitness nor a confidence interval. The dashed Impact-first reference is not an initial chromosome or archive incumbent. **(B)** Lines join each seed's initial-population best and final best at the fixed budget. Diamonds highlight seed 2026103108, selected by minimum planning loss among the 20 archived 100,000-evaluation receipts (32.99552241352315 h); ties within 1e-9 h are resolved by the lowest seed. Initial best losses span 35.238959-36.865575 h and final losses 32.995522-33.003661 h. Panels A/B share a labeled absolute loss scale covering all initial and final values; close final results visually overlap. Pure-random initialization was a post-data methodological choice, not a successful automatic admission under the original rule: only 10/20 runs met the earlier strict proximity cutoff of 32.99884077388271 h, below the registered 90% single-run criterion. Finite-budget reproducibility is not guaranteed near-optimality or global optimality.

**(C)** The empirical cumulative distribution includes all 2,000 paired GA-minus-Impact-first population-weighted normalized service-loss differences from the previously completed fresh candidate-specific physical-validation cohort. Negative values favor GA; 1,741 realizations (87.05%) improve and 259 (12.95%) worsen, with none tied. Black and red vertical lines mark zero and the paired mean, respectively. The inset shows the mean (-0.532674540047 h) and its saved simultaneous 95% confidence interval [-0.563982039, -0.502321315] h, not distributional quantiles. This interval retains the original Bonferroni two-population-contrast family and its already completed 20,000 realization-paired bootstrap resamples; no uncertainty estimate is refitted here. Optimization seeds and physical realizations are different replication units. The separate later post-evaluation-elitism validation cohort is not used.

**(D)** Signed mean differences from the same native event-integrated validation accounting are +0.277475 h for self loss, +0.010638 h for threshold loss, and -0.820788 h for source-path loss, summing to -0.532675 h net. Source-path loss reduction dominates while the other components partly offset it. These are accounting contributions, not identified causal effects of individual station movements. Validation loss integrates normalized modeled service deficit over 0-480 h under the unchanged population mapping and Core-source service gate. The archived planning horizon is 2,855.254 h; its verified completion-bound equivalence to the 480 h planning loss is retained, not silently redefined.

Lower average population burden does not imply improved equity: relative to Impact-first, the mean per-realization absolute SOVI Q4-Q1 burden gap increases by 0.232733 h and population-weighted tract burden Gini by 0.009790 (dimensionless). The service-availability proxy is not delivered MW, verified power flow or actual customers restored. This figure validates one frozen restoration sequence within the model, not universal optimizer superiority or empirical electricity-system accuracy.
