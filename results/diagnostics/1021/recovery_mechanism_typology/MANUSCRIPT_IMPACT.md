# Manuscript implications: proposed wording only

**No change to the October 14 manuscript, numbered figures, formal Stage 7 or
previous 1021 results has been made. This evidence does not independently
justify retaining K3 as a natural community classification.**

## Scientific positioning

Keep earthquake-conditioned disruption, baseline restoration burden and
constraint-related changes central to RQ1/RQ2. For RQ3, describe how those
continuous recovery quantities coexist with frozen vulnerability, population
and hospital-linked geography. Similar modeled recovery can coexist with very
different social vulnerability. Do not require vulnerability to increase with
modeled service deficit, or describe hospital location as all residents' medical
electricity dependence.

If a classification is retained for communication, label it a model-conditioned
descriptive partition. A/K2 is the parsimonious recovery-speed summary. B/K2 is
a different, useful resource-sensitivity summary, not a replacement community
registry. B/K3's 34-tract source-proxy group is not a resilience class. No
particular K is promoted, and no label corresponds automatically to formal P2 IDs.

## Proposed Methods

"We examined recovery-response representations separately from social context.
Tract mean modeled availability was obtained by exact integration of archived
event states over 0-6, 6-24, 24-72, 72-168 and 168-480 h, retaining absolute availability
and equal distance budgets across intervals. A separate mechanism representation
used initial availability, idealized Unconstrained deficit and the matched
Impact-first constrained increment. Clustering and resampling were used to
assess descriptive reproducibility, while unused restoration priorities,
resource conditions and earthquake scenarios provided model-internal transfer
checks. Social vulnerability and population were described after fitting."

## Proposed Results

"Two trajectory groups reproducibly summarized faster and slower modeled
recovery, but offered little additional held-out information beyond simple
burden groupings. A mechanism representation identified tracts with relatively
lower idealized deficit but greater sensitivity to finite repair resources.
Those distinctions depended on restoration priorities, geography and earthquake
scenario, and continuous response models generally preserved more information.
Social-vulnerability distributions overlapped widely within recovery groups."

## Claims supported and not supported

- Supported: some recovery summaries are highly reproducible within the fixed
  experiment; baseline burden and resource sensitivity need not rank tracts alike.
- Supported: a 233-tract B/K2 group has a much larger reduced-crew increment,
  despite only a modest reference-total-burden difference.
- Not supported: three intrinsically discrete community types, a universally
  optimal K, validated actual customer outage classes, or hazard-invariant ranks.
- Not supported: treating the source-proxy initial-state group as physically
  resilient because its clustering is repeatable.
