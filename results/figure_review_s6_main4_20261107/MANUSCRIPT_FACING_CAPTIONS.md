# Manuscript-facing figure captions

## Figure 1. Analytical framework

The workflow distinguishes parallel Unconstrained restoration from resource-constrained priority strategies. At each repair completion, station functionality is updated, source connectivity is reevaluated, and tract service is propagated through the dependency mapping; numerical recovery outcomes are reported in the Results figures.

## Figure 2. Transmission network and tract dependencies

Transmission network, tract-substation mapping, and comparison of candidate tract assignments with public SCE assignments. Assignment agreement does not validate feeder boundaries or recovery outcomes.

## Figure 3. Damage and Unconstrained recovery

Earthquake damage and initial modeled service are followed by Unconstrained recovery. Panel C reports the mean loss shares from substation damage, functionality threshold, and source-path loss; D/E show population-weighted and tract-mean T80. T80 is time to 80% modeled service; applicable whiskers show 5th-95th realization ranges.

## Figure 4. Network-dependent source connectivity during recovery

(A) Recovery of population-dependency-weighted joint functionality and source connection through the full network (solid) or a fixed precomputed path (dashed). (B) Their difference shows restoration-sequence-dependent alternative-route support. (C) Station source access conditional on station functionality; (D) additional conditional reliability beyond the fixed path. Triangles mark Core sources. A/B are dynamic recovery comparisons; C/D are fragility-based station diagnostics. These topological measures are not delivered electricity.

## Figure 5. Restoration-policy performance under 2pc50

Recovery trajectories and outcomes for seven restoration strategies. A shows population-weighted service availability. B reports mean overall, Q4 and hospital-linked service loss and population T80; whiskers show 5th-95th realization ranges. T80 denotes time to 80% modeled service availability.

## Figure 6. Vulnerability-targeted recovery and distributional effects

A/B show overall and quartile service loss. C compares Vulnerability-first with Hospital-first, Impact-first, and Degree-first for overall loss, Q4 loss, signed Q4-Q1 gap, mean within-realization absolute |Q4-Q1| gap, and hospital-linked loss; D shows Gini change and E maps tract-level mean changes. B-D whiskers show 5th-95th realization ranges; negative map values favor Vulnerability-first.

## Figure 7. Resource dependence of policy contrasts under 2pc50

Vulnerability-first effects across crew-availability and repair-duration scenarios. Whiskers are 95% bootstrap confidence intervals for mean effects, not realization ranges; Degree-first hospital-linked comparisons show means only. Negative values indicate lower deficit under Vulnerability-first.

## Figure 8. Community recovery types and social vulnerability

A shows six feature distributions; B shows standardized means for all seven fitted variables, grouped by research question; C shows P2/K3 geography. D maps continuous SOVI and five-policy mean cumulative service deficit; outlined tracts are in frozen FEMA NRI v1.19 SOVI quartile Q4 (the highest social-vulnerability quartile) and have five-policy mean total service deficit at or above 38.38135467381475 equivalent full-service-deficit hours over 0-480 h (the positive-population cohort 75th percentile). The descriptive intersection contains 94 tracts (350,799 residents); high-burden prevalence is 16.23% in Q4 and 27.97% outside Q4. This policy-conditioned screen is not a significance test or intervention-priority score.

## Supplementary Figure S1. Damage and initial service

Station damage severity and corresponding initial modeled tract-service availability. Availability is a network proxy, not physical MW or observed customer outages.

## Supplementary Figure S2. Restoration logistics

Crew bases, station tasks, and task-to-task travel times used in restoration scheduling. These are pre-event scheduling inputs, not post-earthquake traffic forecasts.

## Supplementary Figure S3. Functional-network recovery

Recovery of the largest connected-component fraction and mean degree within that component among functional substations. The curves describe network topology, not power flow or delivered electricity.

## Supplementary Figure S4. Genetic-algorithm diagnostics

A/B summarize 20 GA runs using 64 optimization realizations and 100 repair orders per run. C/D evaluate the selected order on 2,000 separate validation realizations; D decomposes the mean GA-minus-Impact-first loss gap. Negative values indicate lower deficit under GA; a global optimum is not established.

## Supplementary Figure S5. Dependency and service-gate sensitivity

(A) Change in population-weighted normalized service deficit when the M1 mapping cutoff is relaxed from 3% to 0% or 1%. (B) Change when source connectivity is removed while the 0.50 functionality threshold is retained. (C) Change under 0.05 or 0.75 functionality thresholds, with source connectivity reevaluated. Points are means of paired differences across 1,000 frozen physical realizations; bars show empirical 5th-95th percentile ranges, not confidence intervals. No damage samples or repair schedules were regenerated.

## Supplementary Figure S6. Facility planning limits

Provider planning loads, reported limits, and modeled service-loss changes under capacity ceilings. Post-earthquake load flow and complete electrical-capacity adequacy are not assessed.

## Supplementary Figure S7. Restoration-priority geography

Repair-priority geography for Hospital-first, Vulnerability-first, and Impact-first strategies. The white-purple scale shows population; outcome comparisons appear in Figures 5-7 and Supplementary Figure S8.

## Supplementary Figure S8. Outcomes under resource variation

Absolute modeled service losses under crew-availability and repair-duration variations, reported separately for overall, Q4, Q4-Q1, and hospital-linked outcomes. Whiskers show the 5th-95th realization range.
