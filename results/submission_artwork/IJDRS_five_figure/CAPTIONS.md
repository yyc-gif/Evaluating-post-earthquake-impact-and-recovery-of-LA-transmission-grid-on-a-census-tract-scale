# IJDRS five-main-figure captions

## Figure 1. Analytical framework

Five-stage workflow linking seismic damage, source-gated station functionality, tract dependencies, restoration execution and community outcomes. Stage 4 shows an Impact-first priority example, five of 57 crews from 2pc50/C57_D1 evaluation realization 0, and recorded event states. Here 2pc50 denotes 2%-in-50-year ground motion; C57_D1 denotes 57 crews with baseline repair durations. Map numbers are full station priority ranks; scheduling filters damaged tasks. Repair completion releases a crew and restores raw functionality, but source connection and tract service can recover later. Unconstrained is a separate parallel-repair reference. T80 denotes time to 80% modeled service availability.

## Figure 2. Damage-to-service mechanisms and baseline recovery

A shows distributions of mean initial tract service across earthquake scenarios; B maps mean tract T80 under Unconstrained restoration for 2pc50. C separates cumulative service deficit into substation functionality, threshold exclusion and source-path loss. D compares full-network and fixed-path source connection for four focal strategies and Unconstrained; E shows their difference. D/E use 2pc50 and describe population-dependency-weighted joint functionality and source connection, not delivered electricity. Their strategy colors match D's legend. A/C compare four saved earthquake scenarios.

## Figure 3. Restoration performance and distributional trade-offs

For 2pc50/C57_D1. A compares population-weighted overall and Q4 deficits, unweighted hospital-linked tract mean deficit and population T80. B shows quartile-specific loss for four focal strategies. C shows Gini changes; D compares Vulnerability-first with Hospital-first, Impact-first and Degree-first for five outcomes. D distinguishes signed Q4-Q1 separation from mean within-realization absolute |Q4-Q1| separation; neither is a recovery-time difference. C/D are paired strategy contrasts. Points or bars show means; whiskers show empirical 5th-95th realization ranges, not confidence intervals for means. Loss is equivalent full-service-deficit hours over 0-480 h.

## Figure 4. Resource dependence of restoration-policy trade-offs

For 2pc50. Vulnerability-first minus each reference strategy across crew availability and repair-duration multipliers relative to C57_D1. Rows show overall loss, Q4 loss, mean within-realization absolute Q4-Q1 loss gap and hospital-linked loss. Whiskers are 95% bootstrap confidence intervals for mean effects; Degree-first hospital-linked comparisons show means only. Negative changes favor Vulnerability-first for the named outcome, not necessarily for every objective.

## Figure 5. Community recovery types and social vulnerability

For 2pc50/C57_D1. A shows six descriptive distributions; B gives ordinary cohort-standardized means for all seven fitted coordinates; C maps the three community types. Population is fitted as log1p population, while A displays persons. ECDF markers identify median x-coordinates. D maps continuous social vulnerability and mean total deficit across Impact-first, Hospital-first, Degree-first, Betweenness-first and Vulnerability-first. Outlines identify FEMA National Risk Index v1.19 Q4 tracts with deficit at or above the cohort 75th percentile, 38.38135 equivalent hours. Q4 is the highest social-vulnerability quartile. This policy-conditioned descriptive screen is not a significance test or intervention-priority score. Profiles describe fitted differences, not external cluster validation.

## Supplementary Figure S1. Transmission network and tract dependencies

Physical transmission geometry, reduced substation connections, utility-compatible tract mapping and comparison with public Southern California Edison (SCE) assignment records. LADWP denotes Los Angeles Department of Water and Power; CEC denotes California Energy Commission. Assignment agreement does not establish feeder boundaries, power flow or recovery validity.

## Supplementary Figure S2. Damage and Unconstrained recovery-time distributions

A summarizes station-level mean damage states across scenarios: boxes span station interquartile ranges, lines mark medians and whiskers end at observations within the 5th-95th station percentiles, with outliers omitted. B distributes population T80 across 1,000 2pc50 realizations under Unconstrained restoration. The station distribution in A and realization distribution in B have different statistical units. T80 is time to 80% modeled service availability.

## Supplementary Figure S3. Network-dependent source-connectivity diagnostics

For 2pc50. A compares full-network and fixed-path population-dependency-weighted joint functionality/source connection; B gives their difference. Focal and other selected strategies are retained together. The focal dynamic curves also support Figure 2D/E. C shows station source access conditional on functionality; D shows the additional conditional reliability beyond the fixed path. Triangles mark Core sources. A/B are dynamic recovery comparisons; C/D are fragility-based station diagnostics. These are topological quantities, not delivered electricity.

## Supplementary Figure S4. Modeled service-availability trajectories

For 2pc50/C57_D1. Population-weighted service-availability recovery for six scheduled strategies and the idealized Unconstrained reference. Focal and other selected strategies are shown separately with the same time and availability axes. Figure 3A reports the corresponding integrated service deficits and population T80.

## Supplementary Figure S5. Additional distributional and spatial comparisons

For 2pc50/C57_D1. A relates mean overall and Q4 service loss across strategies. B maps tract-level mean Vulnerability-first minus reference service deficits, with Q4 boundaries retained. Negative map values favor Vulnerability-first. The map scale is integrated equivalent deficit hours, not hours earlier or later to a recovery milestone. Figure 3 retains the quartile, paired-outcome and Gini comparisons.

## Supplementary Figure S6. Damage and initial-service geography

Station damage severity and initial modeled tract-service availability across scenarios. Availability is a network proxy, not physical MW or observed customer outages.

## Supplementary Figure S7. Restoration logistics

Crew bases, station tasks and task-to-task travel times used in scheduling. LADWP and SCE identify the two utility organizations. These are pre-event scheduling inputs, not post-earthquake traffic forecasts.

## Supplementary Figure S8. Functional-network recovery

For 2pc50/C57_D1. Time evolution of the largest connected-component fraction and mean degree within that component among functional substations. The curves describe topology, not power flow or delivered electricity.

## Supplementary Figure S9. Genetic-algorithm evaluation

For 2pc50/C57_D1. A/B summarize 20 GA runs using 64 planning realizations and 100 initial repair orders per run. C/D evaluate the selected order on 2,000 held-out simulation realizations; D decomposes the mean GA-minus-Impact-first deficit gap. Negative values favor GA. A global optimum and independent empirical outage validation are not established. Component differences reflect repair timing and gating, not different physical damage samples.

## Supplementary Figure S10. Dependency and service-gate sensitivity

For 2pc50/C57_D1. A relaxes the M1 mapping cutoff from 3% to 0% or 1%. B removes source connectivity while retaining the 0.50 functionality threshold. C changes the threshold to 0.05 or 0.75 with source connectivity reevaluated. Points are means of paired differences across 1,000 saved physical realizations; bars are empirical 5th-95th realization ranges, not confidence intervals. Damage samples and repair schedules are unchanged.

## Supplementary Figure S11. Facility planning limits

Provider planning loads, reported limits and modeled deficit changes under capacity ceilings for 2pc50/C57_D1. B shows population-weighted mean increases and paired 5th-95th realization ranges. Post-earthquake load flow and complete electrical-capacity adequacy are not assessed.

## Supplementary Figure S12. Restoration-priority geography

Hospital-first, Vulnerability-first and Impact-first priority examples. A marks hospital-linked tracts, B marks Q4 tracts and C shades log population. Station ranks are full priority positions, not dispatch positions after damage filtering. Outcomes appear in Figures 3-4 and Supplementary Figures S4, S5 and S13.

## Supplementary Figure S13. Absolute outcomes under resource variation

For 2pc50. Overall, Q4, mean within-realization absolute Q4-Q1 gap and hospital-linked deficits across crew and repair-duration multipliers relative to C57_D1. Whiskers show empirical 5th-95th realization ranges. These absolute outcomes complement Figure 4's paired mean effects and bootstrap intervals.
