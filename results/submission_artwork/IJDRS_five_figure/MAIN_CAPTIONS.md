# IJDRS main-figure captions

## Figure 1. Analytical framework

Five-stage workflow linking seismic damage, source-gated station functionality, tract dependencies, restoration execution and community outcomes. Stage 4 shows an Impact-first priority example, five of 57 crews from 2pc50/C57_D1 evaluation realization 0, and recorded event states. Here 2pc50 denotes 2%-in-50-year ground motion; C57_D1 denotes 57 crews with baseline repair durations. Map numbers are full station priority ranks; scheduling filters damaged tasks. Repair completion releases a crew and restores raw functionality, but source connection and tract service can recover later. Unconstrained is a separate parallel-repair reference. T80 denotes time to 80% modeled service availability.

## Figure 2. Damage-to-service mechanisms and baseline recovery

A shows distributions of mean initial tract service across earthquake scenarios; B maps mean tract T80 under Unconstrained restoration for 2pc50. C separates cumulative service deficit into substation functionality, threshold exclusion and source-path loss. D compares full-network and fixed-path source connection for four focal strategies and Unconstrained; E shows their difference. D/E use 2pc50 and describe population-dependency-weighted joint functionality and source connection, not delivered electricity. Their strategy colors match D's legend. A/C compare four saved earthquake scenarios.

## Figure 3. Restoration performance and distributional trade-offs

For 2pc50/C57_D1. A compares population-weighted overall and Q4 deficits, unweighted hospital-linked tract mean deficit and population T80. B shows quartile-specific loss for four focal strategies. C shows Gini changes; D compares Vulnerability-first with Hospital-first, Impact-first and Degree-first for five outcomes. D distinguishes signed Q4-Q1 separation from mean within-realization absolute |Q4-Q1| separation; neither is a recovery-time difference. C/D are paired strategy contrasts. Points or bars show means; whiskers show empirical 5th-95th realization ranges, not confidence intervals for means. Loss is equivalent full-service-deficit hours over 0-480 h.

## Figure 4. Resource dependence of restoration-policy trade-offs

For 2pc50. Vulnerability-first minus each reference strategy across crew availability and repair-duration multipliers relative to C57_D1. Rows show overall loss, Q4 loss, mean within-realization absolute Q4-Q1 loss gap and hospital-linked loss. Whiskers are 95% bootstrap confidence intervals for mean effects; Degree-first hospital-linked comparisons show means only. Negative changes favor Vulnerability-first for the named outcome, not necessarily for every objective.

## Figure 5. Community recovery types and social vulnerability

For 2pc50/C57_D1. A shows six descriptive distributions; B gives ordinary cohort-standardized means for all seven fitted coordinates; C maps the three community types. Population is fitted as log1p population, while A displays persons. ECDF markers identify median x-coordinates. D maps continuous social vulnerability and mean total deficit across Impact-first, Hospital-first, Degree-first, Betweenness-first and Vulnerability-first. Outlines identify FEMA National Risk Index v1.19 Q4 tracts with deficit at or above the cohort 75th percentile, 38.38135 equivalent hours. Q4 is the highest social-vulnerability quartile. This policy-conditioned descriptive screen is not a significance test or intervention-priority score. Profiles describe fitted differences, not external cluster validation.
