## Association and social vulnerability

**Baseline and added modeled service deficits across Los Angeles census tracts.** The analysis covers 2,315 tracts under the earthquake scenario with 2% probability of exceedance in 50 years, over a 480 h evaluation period and at the reference restoration resource level. Baseline is the idealized unconstrained recovery deficit. Added deficit is the equal mean of the five constrained policy deficits minus the matched unconstrained deficit. **A** shows tract density on a logarithmic color scale. Dashed lines mark each burden's domain-wide 75th percentile. **B–C** show the distributions across fixed social vulnerability quartiles (Q1 lowest, Q4 highest). Thin bars show the 10th–90th percentiles across tracts, thick bars show the 25th–75th percentiles, open circles show tract medians, and diamonds show population-weighted means. The bars summarize the distribution of tract averages. [Candidate image](association_and_vulnerability.png)

## Geographic overlap of the highest burdens

**Spatial overlap of the highest baseline and added modeled service deficits.** Colors show which of 2,315 Los Angeles census tracts reach the domain-wide 75th percentile of baseline deficit (35.08 h), added deficit (5.44 h), both, or neither. For the earthquake scenario with 2% probability of exceedance in 50 years and the reference resource level, the baseline is idealized unconstrained recovery. Added deficit is the equal mean of the five constrained policy deficits minus the matched baseline. Both deficits are integrated over 480 h. The intersection represents 761,166 residents (8.4% of the study population). These are burden quartiles derived from tract means. [Candidate image](top_burden_overlap.png)
