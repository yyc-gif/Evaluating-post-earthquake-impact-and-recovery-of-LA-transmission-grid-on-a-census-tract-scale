# Which communities do deficit screens miss?

Independent LA grid research update for Marta | 23 November 2026

## Finding

At the reference restoration resource level, tracts with the highest modeled service deficit under idealized unconstrained recovery were often different from those with the largest extra deficit under constrained repair. With the same number of places on each list, total deficit covered more of the high added deficit group, but still missed more than two in five tracts.

## Equal size screening across 2,315 tracts

| Screen ranked by | High added deficit tracts found | Tracts missed | Residents in missed tracts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Baseline deficit | 212 of 579 (36.6%) | 367 | 1,432,501 |
| Total deficit | 334 of 579 (57.7%) | 245 | 963,079 |

The target is the top quarter by the mean added deficit over five repair priorities. For individual priorities, baseline coverage ranged from 20.4% to 62.5%, and coverage using the corresponding total deficit ranged from 51.3% to 76.5%. For the five-priority mean, the coverage advantage persisted at the top 20%, 25%, and 30% and in all 47 spatial block omissions. Total deficit contains the added deficit, so this is a comparison of rankings computed from the same simulations.

## Why the average hides the strategy difference

The Pearson correlation between baseline and mean added deficit was 0.027. Degree-first contributed +2.111 hours squared to the covariance of the five-priority mean, while the other four contributions summed to -1.764 hours squared. After omitting Degree-first and recomputing the mean, Pearson r was -0.162. This offset is why the near-zero mean correlation tells us little about any one strategy.

## What this adds to the paper

The draft already shows unequal unconstrained recovery, maps high total deficit alongside social vulnerability, and describes community types formed using both burden measures. This comparison counts who falls outside the baseline and total deficit screens when the question is where the model adds the most burden under constrained recovery. The counts refer to residents of ranked tracts. They do not measure observed outages or isolate crew, travel, or repair duration effects.

## Point for discussion

Could this be a tract-level result within the existing third research question? The contribution would be the number and population of tracts omitted under a fixed screening limit, and how that omission depends on repair priority. The analysis covers the earthquake scenario with 2% probability of exceedance in 50 years and the reference resource level. Closely related work on LA load shedding and hurricane restoration already distinguishes aspects of burden and intervention benefit. Full-text checks of the closest LA journal studies and a broader search for earlier equal-size screening studies remain open.
