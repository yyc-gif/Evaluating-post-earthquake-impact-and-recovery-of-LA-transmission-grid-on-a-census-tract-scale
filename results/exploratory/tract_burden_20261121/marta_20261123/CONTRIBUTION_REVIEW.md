# Contribution review and source checks

## Contribution for discussion

**At the same tract screening limit, ranking by unconstrained or total modeled service deficit identifies different communities from ranking by added deficit. The degree of overlap depends on which repair priority is modeled.** The main value is a quantified omission cost by tract and resident count for an explicitly defined target. This is evidence about the choice of a model output for screening. The identity of the missed tracts and the absolute counts are conditional on the study domain, matched reference, earthquake scenario, resource level, priorities, and threshold.

The stronger candidate is a short result within the existing third research question. The contrast between the Hospital-first and Degree-first screens helps establish why the five-priority mean is insufficient. A difference in overlap does not rank the strategies by service performance, fairness, or operational value. Agreement between total and added rankings is partly built into their definitions, and a total-deficit screen can serve its own purpose even when it misses tracts with a large increment.

## Findings to keep within scope

- The five-priority covariance is +0.347 h². The four negative terms offset 83.5% of the +2.111 h² Degree-first term. The absolute net is 9.0% of the sum of the five absolute terms. With population weighting, the corresponding terms are +2.105 and −1.583 h², leaving +0.522 h². The shared baseline enters every increment. It cannot establish independence, causal mechanisms, or the relationship for a randomly selected policy.
- Population-weighted Q4 minus Q1 is −1.424 h for baseline deficit and −1.568 h for mean added deficit. The evidence does not support reversed vulnerability gradients. Population counts in omitted tracts are coverage figures, without an implied count of disconnected customers.
- Equal-size rank comparisons and spatial leave-block-out ranges are descriptive. The ranges are not confidence intervals or an out-of-sample validation. Taking out one policy averages the four already-simulated outcomes. It does not remove that priority from an operational dispatch procedure or alter a schedule.
