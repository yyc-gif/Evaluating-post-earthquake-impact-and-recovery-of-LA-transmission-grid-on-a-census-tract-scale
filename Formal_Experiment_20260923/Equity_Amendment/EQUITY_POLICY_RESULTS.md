# Reviewer-directed equity policy amendment: formal results

## Direct findings

`vulnerability-first` is a fixed, ex-ante 92-station priority order derived solely from the already frozen highest-vulnerability (Q4) tracts: `V_s = sum_{r in Q4} Population_r × W_M1[r,s]`, sorted by descending `V_s`, descending total mapped population, then ascending station ID. It uses the existing NRI-derived `SOVI_SCORE` quartiles, not CDC SVI. This is a transparent *equity-informed targeting comparator*, not a disparity optimizer. Its sequence and provenance are in `VULNERABILITY_FIRST_SEQUENCE.json` (sequence SHA256 `7473a0a8170ed5bb40cdd0f1e13d3c55723acc6809ce5e2768a5f4e4fa5d717f`). The pre-execution rule was committed and pushed before scientific execution.

Exactly 10,000 complete vulnerability-first trajectories were added: 4,000 baseline C57/D1 (1,000 per frozen hazard) and 6,000 2pc50 one-factor-at-a-time resources (C29/C86/C114 and D0.75/D1.25/D1.50). They reuse the original 4,000 evaluation physical samples; no DS, duration, GA, existing strategy, production mapping/gate, or original 84,000 trajectories was rerun. The 480 h common horizon was retained. A first attempt completed science for one Northridge case but could not write its task-event CSV because of a Windows path-length error; the partial output and failure record remain, and the short-path complete archive was written once. Thus there are 10,000 **complete** new trajectories and one failed/incomplete write attempt; the latter is not counted as an additional formal result. Compact identities, logistics, paired effects, and tract effects are linked by `VULNERABILITY_TRAJECTORY_INDEX.json` and `VULNERABILITY_RESULTS_INDEX.json`.

Under 2pc50/C57, the Q4 absolute mean modeled burden is **32.023 h**, versus 33.618 h for hospital-first and 32.733 h for impact-first. Paired differences are **−1.595 h** (95% paired bootstrap interval −1.631 to −1.558) and **−0.710 h**, respectively. This Q4 gain is real within the fixed model, but Q1 rises by **+1.269 h** versus hospital-first and **+2.162 h** versus impact-first; Q2 also rises (+0.740/+1.335 h), while Q3 falls versus hospital-first (−0.407 h) and rises slightly versus impact-first (+0.081 h). The signed Q4−Q1 gap becomes more negative by about 2.86–2.87 h. **The absolute Q4−Q1 gap increases by +2.50 h versus hospital-first and +2.54 h versus impact-first**, and Gini rises by +0.0050/+0.0059. A signed-gap reduction is therefore *not* a general reduction in group separation or inequality; it includes material delay to the formerly lower-burden Q1 group. The complete Q1–Q4 absolute values and paired intervals are in `VULNERABILITY_GROUP_SUMMARY.csv`, `VULNERABILITY_PAIRWISE_EFFECTS.csv`, and Figure B.

Versus hospital-first, vulnerability-first changes aggregate population burden by only **−0.049 h** (paired interval −0.079 to −0.019), while population T80 increases **+0.466 h** and hospital-tract burden **+0.572 h**. The tiny aggregate mean difference should not be presented as a large efficiency gain or as proof of equivalence. Versus impact-first, population burden increases **+0.664 h**, T80 **+1.186 h**, and hospital burden **+0.880 h**. Hospital burden is only a modeled service-access proxy for tracts containing retained hospital records, not hospital operating capacity. Population burden is modeled normalized service deficit, not observed resident outage hours.

On the 2pc50 paired-mean tract classification relative to hospital-first, **328 tracts/1,325,640 people improve**, **532/2,104,575 worsen**, and **1,455/5,636,307 are within ±1 h**. Among Q4, 127 tracts/551,450 people improve, 31/139,034 worsen, and 421/1,709,222 are near zero. Relative to impact-first, 318 tracts/1,243,921 people improve and 589/2,342,319 worsen; Q4 improvement covers 441,462 people and worsening 180,130. These categories use a *practical*, not statistical, ±1 h threshold. Per-realization classification averaged over 1,000 realizations is separately retained in `VULNERABILITY_CLASSIFICATION_POPULATION.csv`; it is not interchangeable with classification of each tract's paired mean effect. All 2,315 mapped tracts have resolved mass in this July92 mapping, hence unresolved population is zero in this amendment; the `unresolved` category remains explicit and would be NA rather than zero burden if present.

Across Northridge, San Fernando, and Long Beach, Q4 gains versus hospital-first are −0.004, −0.009, and −0.015 h; versus impact-first they are −0.033, −0.016, and −0.013 h. These are directionally similar but extremely small in absolute time. The population burden difference versus hospital-first is +0.006, −0.001, and −0.003 h across those hazards. The 2pc50 tradeoff must not be generalized as an equally large historical-hazard effect. Figures A and B and the full paired table show all four hazards.

The resource response is not invariant. In 2pc50 **C29**, vulnerability-first lowers Q4 burden versus hospital-first by **6.385 h** but raises aggregate population burden **1.710 h**, population T80 **6.255 h**, and the absolute Q4−Q1 gap **15.268 h**. Against impact-first at C29 the Q4 gain is 2.727 h with a 3.809 h population-burden cost. At C86/C114, mean Q4 and aggregate differences between vulnerability-first and hospital-first are close to zero; abundant crews leave less scheduling leverage. At C57 with durations ×0.75/×1.25/×1.5, Q4 gains versus hospital-first are 1.203/1.986/2.377 h, while absolute-gap increases are 1.906/3.096/3.691 h. These one-factor-at-a-time contrasts reuse the same physical DS vectors and scaled saved durations; they are not a crew×duration factorial experiment. Full paired effects and changes from baseline are in `VULNERABILITY_RESOURCE_EFFECTS.csv` and Figure D.

## Objective and gate interpretation

The July GA optimized weighted early **station** completion less makespan; its candidate fitness did not propagate to source-gated tract burden. The revised direct GA optimizes the negative mean aggregate population-times-resolved-mass burden across **64 independent planning damage/workload realizations under the same fixed 2pc50 PGA field**, using completion-event scheduling, the production source gate, and M1. Seven fixed rules were scored first; impact-first was the best deterministic incumbent. None of five fixed-budget GA seeds improved it, so `direct-community` and impact-first are the **same sequence and decision**. The new vulnerability-first rule is the only explicit Q4-targeted comparator. Precise Methods text is in `GA_METHODS_CLARIFICATION.md`; no GA code, objective, search, planning sample, or incumbent was changed here.

The separate no-threshold-connectivity audit read all 84,000 retained formal trajectories plus the 10,000 complete amendment trajectories: **717,233,104 known event×station cells**, minimum functionality 0.03 and minimum Core-source functionality 0.03, with zero missing or nonpositive cells. The retained 92-station/318-edge graph is one source-containing component. Under `C_positive` computed on all `f>0` stations, every saved state remains connected to an active Core source; therefore `f × C_positive = f` and the proposed `G_NO_THRESHOLD_CONNECTIVITY` is *exactly the existing G0 no-gate case*. It adds no independent scientific scenario. The production 0.5 threshold remains unchanged: it decides whether a damaged asset can transmit topological connectivity. Removing it is not a continuous-capacity model; that would require validated damage-to-transfer-capacity, branch/transformer ratings, and electrical constraints. The machine record is `NO_THRESHOLD_CONNECTIVITY_EQUIVALENCE.json`. Existing dynamic redundancy results remain descriptive of binary paths, not MW capacity.
